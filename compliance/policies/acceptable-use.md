# Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)

## TL;DR
The AUP defines how employees may (and may not) use company systems, data, and networks. It sets behavioral expectations, establishes the basis for enforcement, and is a common baseline control across frameworks. Everyone signs it (onboarding + periodically).

## Purpose
```text
- Set clear boundaries for using company IT (devices, accounts, data, network, cloud).
- Provide legal/HR basis to act on misuse; support monitoring notice.
- Reduce risk from risky behavior (shadow IT, oversharing, insecure practices).
```

## What it typically covers
```text
- Scope: who + which systems/data/networks (incl. BYOD, remote, cloud/SaaS).
- Acceptable use: business purpose; limited personal use (if allowed).
- Prohibited: unauthorized access, sharing credentials, installing unapproved software,
  disabling security controls, illegal content, circumventing monitoring.
- Data handling: classification, no exfil to personal accounts/USB, encryption expectations.
- Email/web/messaging use; social media conduct; AI/LLM tool usage (increasingly relevant).
- Monitoring notice: systems may be monitored; no expectation of privacy on company systems.
- Reporting: how to report security incidents / suspected misuse.
- Consequences: disciplinary action up to termination; legal referral.
```

## Related policies to reference
```text
- Data classification & handling, access control, remote work/BYOD, password/MFA,
  clean desk, incident reporting. See policy-templates.
```

## Best practices
```text
- Plain language, concise, and role-relevant; avoid an unreadable legal wall.
- Signed acknowledgment on hire + on major updates; track completion (audit evidence).
- Pair with awareness training (the AUP explains the rules; training builds the habit).
- Keep current: add emerging topics (AI tools, cloud/SaaS, personal-device use).
- Review annually; owner accountable.
```
